    Before you start

    Eight habits that change the answer

    ثماني عادات تغيّر النتيجة

    The difference between a generic answer and a useful one is almost always in the prompt.

    1

    Set the role

    “You are a health communicator writing for the public” beats no role at all.

    2

    Be specific about the output

    Say the format, the length and the audience — “one page, for leadership, under 300 words”.

    3

    Ground it on your data

    Attach the file or point at the thread. Otherwise it answers from the internet.

    4

    Iterate, don’t restart

    “Shorter, and lead with the recommendation” gets you further than a brand-new prompt.

    5

    Ask it to show its work

    Especially for anything with numbers in it. Then actually check the working.

    6

    Give a style example

    Paste something you liked and say “match this tone”.

    7

    Chain, don’t cram

    One job per prompt, then build on the answer: research → analyse → draft → present.

    8

    Verify high-stakes work

    Clinical, financial or public-facing? You own the final check, not the model.

    Analyst — reasoning over your data

    Drop in a spreadsheet and ask what is going on. Analyst reasons step by step, runs the code in front of you, and shows its working. Two synthetic practice workbooks come with this pack — use those today, not real files.

    Tier 02 · Specialist agents

    The one that convinces the sceptics

    Because you can watch the code and check it. The practice files are deliberately long and a little messy, with real patterns buried inside — so there is something genuine to find rather than a tidy table that answers itself.

    👀 Watch for during the demoBoth files contain deliberate imperfections — a few blank scores, and rows where attendance exceeds registration. Watch whether Copilot notices them unprompted. If it does not, ask it to check data quality and watch it find them.
    Try these variants
    • “What is the single most surprising thing in this file?”
    • “What would you need to know before trusting this analysis?”
    • “Compare the two files — do the patterns agree?”
